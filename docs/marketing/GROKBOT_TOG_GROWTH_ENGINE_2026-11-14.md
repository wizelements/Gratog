### A. Define the economic objective

Revenue is not success if acquisition, supplies, labor, merchant fees, spoilage, delivery, customer support and discounts leave little or negative profit. Use actual costs, not assumptions; mark missing data UNKNOWN. Define the business-level scorecard:

- **Contribution profit** = paid net sales (exclude tax and refunds; discount applied) − variable ingredients − bottle/jar/label/packaging costs − payment fees − production labor − market/delivery variable expenses − attributable marketing/discount expense − spoilage.
- **Repeat customer rate** by 30/60/90-day cohort; distinguish unique, consenting identities from anonymous walk-ins.
- **Contribution profit per market hour**, **revenue per batch**, **waste %**, **stockout rate**, **net AOV**, **order completion**, **fulfillment correctness**, **first-to-second purchase**, **customer complaints/refunds**, **consented weekly list quality**, **qualified event bookings**, **attributed profit per campaign**.
- **Incrementality:** compare campaigns against a holdout or comparable weeks while recording weather, event attendance, menu, inventory and seasonality; distinguish observational correlation from causal impact.
- **Operator burden:** minutes spent on marketing, fulfillment, rework and exceptions per sale; brand success must increase capacity, not trap Jenneisha in more manual work.

Make a 14-day instrumented baseline before establishing targets. Proposed 90-day goals are *hypotheses* to revisit after baseline: increase contribution profit and 60-day repeat purchase, reduce order drop-offs, eliminate price/catalog mismatches, achieve zero unauthorized marketing sends, and eliminate false availability promises. Do not invent baseline numbers, ROI, visitor counts, follower counts or customer counts.

### D. Psychological strategy — tested, ethical and local

Classify every tactic by (1) evidence from credible research or UX guidance, (2) applicability to small-batch food, (3) implementation cost, (4) legal/reputation risk, (5) experiment to measure actual lift. Do not label a tactic 'scientifically guaranteed.'

- **Sensory language:** real flavor/aroma/color/temperature/texture and recognizable ingredients (e.g. bright citrus; cooling mint; tropical guava). Do not promise a physiological outcome.
- **Cognitive ease:** first-screen 'Explore flavors', 'Order for yourself', 'Preorder Serenbe pickup', 'Plan an event' with fulfillment availability and clear prices, not 8 conflicting CTAs.
- **Authentic narrative:** founder voice, full verified Ted's Gym history, behind-the-scenes prep and real local market conversations; avoid generic AI writing and invented struggles.
- **Reciprocity:** real samples, helpful tasting notes, clear allergen answers. Sampling does not entitle the brand to contact permission.
- **Honest social proof:** permissioned reviews and UGC; no manufactured testimonials, fake customer counts, purchased reviews or undisclosed influencer gifts.
- **Real scarcity/novelty:** actual batch quantities and cutoff timestamps; never fake urgency or evergreen limited badges.
- **Relevant choice architecture:** guide first-time buyers via a 2-question voluntary flavor finder; recommendations must respect available stock, dietary restrictions, ingredients and separate bundle rules.
- **Meaningful ritual:** weekly fresh-menu communication, clearly opt-in; a recognition and reorder moment at Serenbe.
- **Identity/belonging:** gratitude as community practice, but never guilt customers into spending or prey on health anxieties.
- **Personalization:** use volunteered taste preferences and previous purchases only with appropriate lawful permissions and opt-outs. Avoid inferred health conditions or sensitive traits.

Scientific/industry starting points: Chernev et al. meta-analysis on choice overload; 2026 Journal of Marketing meta-analysis on personalization; Nielsen Norman Group product-page and selling-strategy UX findings; FTC health products guidance, endorsements and fake reviews rules; Google Business Profile local-ranking guidance. Verify each source for applicability; do not pretend A/B tests from another industry prove local conversion.

### I. Research/legal guardrails

- **FTC:** Health claims must be truthful, nonmisleading and appropriately substantiated, including implied claims; avoid disease/medical benefit messaging. A boilerplate FDA disclaimer cannot fix a misleading ad.
- **FTC reviews:** Real, representative experiences only, required material-connection disclosure and no rating-gated incentives, fake testimonials or purchased followers.
- **CAN-SPAM:** accurate commercial email headers, truthful subjects, a valid postal contact, functioning opt-out and prompt suppression; transactional communications must not be repurposed for marketing.
- **TCPA / Square SMS:** prior, appropriate marketing text consent, recording consent context, support STOP/opt-out and do not interpret a checkout phone field as permission for promotional texts.
- **Food safety:** disclose actual ingredients/allergens and shared equipment risks where applicable; avoid advertising unverified allergy-safe preparation.
- **Accessibility:** readable mobile type, keyboard/focus paths, meaningful labels, WCAG testing and no hidden contact information.
- **Brand:** original, specific, grateful, inviting, sensory, everyday human language; no robotic prose, exaggerated luxury language, guilt, fear or miracle-health themes.
